# The campaign: a plan

A plan, not yet built. It sets out a story mode beside free play: what the story is, what
the player has to keep up with (a clock, weather, food, fuel, trucks on finance and a
mortgage), what has to be made, and in what order. Numbers are starting points to be tuned
by playing. What is decided, and by whom, is marked; the open questions are at the end.

## Two ways to play

| | Free play | Story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maps | All of them, from the start | Opened as you can afford to reach them |
| Time and weather | Chosen at each trailhead, as now | Their own: the clock runs, the weather comes as it comes |
| Truck | Any in the catalogue, in any colour | What you own and are paying off |
| Money, food, fuel | None of it matters | All of it matters |
| Kept game | `save/free.txt` | `save/story.txt`, one story at a time |

Free play is the game as it is today, and stays: *any truck, on any map* (decided
2026-11-15). The title screen offers both, and the two keep separate games.

## The story: making it on your own

*Decided (2026-11-15): you start from nothing and want to make it on your own. No
inheritance, nobody's reef to find. People can come later. **There is no winning**: the
story has no end, and more is added to it as it is made, different 4x4s above all.*

**Length.** Five to ten hours to have seen everything there is at first; no end.

**The premise.** You have nothing and you have decided to try gold. You sign for two
things on the first day, with no money down:

- **The block at the Crossroads**: a shed on a few acres at the end of the main road, with
  a trailhead to every track. $100,000, on a mortgage.
- **A truck**: one of three old 4x4s at about $10,000 each, on finance.

So the game opens **$110,000 in debt**, with a tank of fuel and a detector. Everything
after that is yours to earn: the repayments, the food, the fuel, a better truck when the
bank will lend you one, harder country when the truck can reach it. Paying the mortgage
out is the biggest thing to have done, and it ends nothing: there is always another truck
to own, and more is added.

**What gives it a shape.** With nobody in it yet, the story is told by what you can do and
what you owe. These are not chapters that end: they are what a player will have done, in
about this order, each something to point to.

| | Where | What it asks | The thing to point to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Signed up* | The Crossroads, Ironbark Flat | Choose a truck, find a first nugget, sell it, buy food and fuel. | The first week's repayments made |
| *Making payments* | Wombat Creek Track | Detecting for a living: fords, the weather, a day's food. | Credit good enough for a second-hand truck worth having |
| *Something better* | Granite Ridge | The ridge wants a truck that can climb it, or the old one built up in your own garage. | A truck paid off, or traded up |
| *The wet* | Blackwood Gully | The season turns. Mud, the winch, bigger gold near the creeks, bigger bills. | The mortgage under half |
| *Clear* | Everywhere, and the Lost Reef | The best ground is the hardest to reach. | The block is yours |
| *After* | Wherever is added | A shed full of trucks. | Whatever you want next |

The Lost Reef is a last, small map of rich ground behind a creek that is only low enough to
cross after a dry spell. It is there as the place a built truck and a good forecast are
*for*, not as somebody's secret. (Open question 1: is that enough of a story, or do you want
events along the way: a flood, a mining company pegging ground you have not worked, an
offer for the block?)

**Losing.** There is no losing either. Nothing ends the story. A missed repayment costs credit and adds to the
debt; a truck two payments behind is repossessed and you are sold the cheapest one on the
lot; the block is never taken (open question 3).

## What the story changes about the game

### The clock, and travel

*Decided: in the story the time and the weather cannot be set, the weather is its own, and
going from map to map takes time.*

- The story's clock is its own. A game day is 48 minutes: daylight is about 25 of them.
  (Free play keeps the clock it has, set at the trailhead.)
- The trailhead's card shows the time, the weather and the forecast, and offers no choice.
- **Going somewhere takes time**, and fuel:

  | From the Crossroads to | Time | Fuel |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Ironbark Flat | 25 min | 4 L |
  | Wombat Creek Track | 40 min | 7 L |
  | Granite Ridge | 1 h 15 | 13 L |
  | Blackwood Gully | 1 h 30 | 15 L |
  | The Lost Reef, from Blackwood | 50 min | 9 L |

  Fuel for a truck that drinks more is more: the table is for a small diesel.
- **Sleeping** in the shed passes the night and ends the day.

### The weather

- One weather for the whole country, made from the story's seed an hour at a time: fronts
  come through over a day or two, and the season turns from dry late autumn to the wet.
- A **forecast** for two days, at the store and on the radio. Right most of the time.
- Rain raises the fords, softens the bog holes and lays the dust. The day after heavy rain
  the creek banks have **fresh ground**: more gold near water. Bad weather is a reason to
  go as well as a risk.

### Food

*Decided: not eating costs a day and $100.*

One rule. You eat once a day: food bought at the store, kept in the tray or the shed as
things, and eaten from the hand. **End a day without having eaten and the next day is
lost** (you are no use to anyone), **and $100 goes on the debt.** The repayments do not
wait for the lost day.

No water or tiredness meters. (They were in the first plan; this is simpler and was asked
for.)

### Debt, and your credit

*Decided: about $100,000 for the block and $10,000 for the truck, and a credit rating that
decides what trucks you can get.*

- **Two loans** from the start: the mortgage on the block and the finance on the truck.
  Each has a repayment that comes out every Friday.

  | | Owed | A week |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | The block | $100,000 | $1,500 |
  | A $10,000 truck | $10,000 | $350 |

  Anything can be paid down early at the credit union's counter in town.
- **A credit rating**, from 0 to 100, shown on the bills page. It starts low: you have
  just borrowed $110,000 with no history.

  | It goes up for | It goes down for |
  | --- | --- |
  | Every Friday paid in full | A Friday missed or short |
  | Paying a loan out | A truck repossessed |
  | Owing less than you did | A day lost for not eating ($100 on the debt) |

- **What it decides**: how much truck the dealer will finance, and at what rate.

  | Rating | Called | The dealer will finance |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0 to 39 | Poor | Up to $12,000: the three you started with a choice of |
  | 40 to 59 | Fair | Up to $30,000 |
  | 60 to 79 | Good | Up to $70,000 |
  | 80 to 100 | Excellent | Anything on the lot |

  Cash buys anything at any rating.

### Trucks

*Decided (2026-11-15): the trucks are our own, modelled in Blender, in pieces. Fifty of
them: ten in each of five grades. Each is inspired by the kinds of 4x4 there are, with
features mixed between makers, and none is a real make or model. You choose a first one
from three at about $10,000; colours and parts are changed in your own garage.*

**They are made by a script** (`tools/blender/truck/build.py`), as the shed and the town
are. A truck is a set of numbers: how long, wide and high, where its cab sits, how its
screen leans, what its back is (tray, tub or wagon); and a choice from each list of parts.
`blender -b --python tools/blender/truck/build.py -- all OUT_FOLDER KEEP_FOLDER` builds
every one, each to a file of its own, with a picture.

**In pieces.** Every part that could be changed is an object of its own in the truck's
file, named for what it is, so that the game can take one off and fit another:

| | Pieces |
| --- | --- |
| Body | `nose`, `guard_L/R` (where they are separate), `bonnet`, `cab`, `door_FL/FR/RL/RR`, `tub` or `tray` or a wagon's back, `tailgate`, `canopy`, `tonneau` |
| Face | `grille`, `lamp_head_L/R`, `indicator_L/R`, `bumper_front`, `plate_front` |
| Glass | `glass_screen`, `glass_back`, `glass_quarter_L/R` (a door's is the door's) |
| Tail | `lamp_tail_L/R`, `bumper_rear`, `plate_rear`, `flap_L/R` |
| Sides | `flare_..` or `lip_..` round each arch, `trim_L/R`, `step_L/R`, `mirror_L/R`, `filler`, `aerial`, `wipers` |
| Under | `chassis`, `axle_front`, `axle_rear`, `exhaust` |
| Wheels | `wheel_FL/FR/RL/RR`, each about its own hub; `spare` |
| Bolted on | `bullbar`, `snorkel`, `rack`, `lightbar`, `sportsbar` |

**Parts come in kinds**, and no two trucks have the same set:

| Part | Kinds |
| --- | --- |
| Grille | slats, bars, mesh, egg-crate, split, a pressed panel with slots, one wide bar, a heavy frame |
| Head lamps | round, twin round, square, twin square, wide, round the corner, one over another, on the guards |
| Front bumper | chrome blade, steel channel, tube, moulded, sport with a skid plate, winch plate |
| Bonnet | flat, bulged, with a scoop, with vents |
| Guards | joined to the bonnet, or separate from it |
| Arches | round or square; bare, lipped, with bolted flares or moulded ones |
| Rims | eight-slot steel, sunraysia, five-spoke, six-spoke, mesh, beadlock; steel, white, black or bronze |
| Tyres | road, all-terrain, mud |
| Sides | plain, a rubbing strip, cladding, a stripe, two-tone |
| Mirrors | on a stalk, on two arms, a pod on the door |
| Roof | plain, ribbed, guttered |
| Tray | alloy, timber, with tool boxes under it |
| Tub | plain, with a sports bar, a tonneau or a canopy |
| Bar work | tube bar, winch bar, nudge bar; with or without spot lamps |

**The grades**, and what a truck in each costs second-hand:

| Grade | | Price | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Paddock bashers | $6,000 to $11,000 | Dargo (tray-back ute), Omeo (tiny wagon), Licola (short flat-panelled tray) |
| 2 | Old tourers | $14,000 to $30,000 | Howitt (big wagon on coils), Jamieson (flat cab and tray), Mitta |
| 3 | Modern workhorses | $34,000 to $58,000 | Ovens (dual-cab ute), Buffalo, Hotham, Tumut |
| 4 | Built tourers | $52,000 to $105,000 | Jamieson V8, Bogong Sport, Ovens Tourer |
| 5 | The top end | $120,000 to $255,000 | Kestrel (full-size desert truck), Cobbler, Stirling, Wellington |

They are named for places in the high country. The three to start with are from grade 1.
All fifty, with pictures, are in a catalogue (the Truck Yard artifact).

**Where they stand.** The shapes are a first pass: the line-up, the proportions and the
parts are there, and they are plainer than the Raptor, which is 380,000 triangles of
somebody's hand work with a whole cab inside. What is still to make them good: an inside
(seats, a dash, a wheel) behind glass that is seen through, panel creases and swage lines,
proper lamp lenses, badges of their own, and textures for tyres, glass and trim. Each of
those is added to the script once and every truck gets it.

**Their numbers.** Each truck's `.vehicle` file (weight, springs, engine, gears, which
axles lock) is still to be written, from the kind of truck it is; its wheelbase, track and
tyre size come from the same numbers its model is built from.

**Colour, rust, dust and mud** are all the truck's *coat* (`Coat` in `solstice-render`,
`Wear` in `apps/viewer/src/play.rs`; a first version works, 2026-11-15). None of it is a
texture. Where the truck is drawn, each point of it is worked out in the truck's own space
(how high it is, how near a wheel, which way it faces) from a few numbers the game holds,
so any of it can change from one frame to the next and none of it is downloaded:

- **Paint**: a colour. A model's paint is a material whose name begins `paint`; what in
  its texture is some other colour (a stripe, a badge) is left as it is. Chosen in the
  garage.
- **Rust**, 0 to 1: low on the panels and round the wheel arches first, in blotches that
  spread and run. An old truck comes with some; it is cut out and painted over in the
  garage, and comes back slowly.
- **Dust**, 0 to 1: a film over everything, thickest on what faces up and towards the
  back. A minute of dry road is a dusty truck.
- **Mud**, 0 to 1: caked from the ground up, thrown up behind each wheel, spattered above
  that, and all over the wheels. It goes on wet and dark and dries pale in a few minutes.
  A bog hole is a muddy truck in a few lengths.
- A ford washes what it reaches, rain washes all of it slowly, and the garage has a hose.

What is in the cab and under the bonnet (materials named `inside...`) takes no dirt. Each
truck's own rust and spatter come from a number of its own. Still to do: keep it with the
truck in the kept game, a wash, each truck's own paint colour and wheel size from its file,
and more than one truck wearing a coat at once (a second in the shed).

**The garage** is your shed. Parked in it, a truck can be changed:

- its **colour**;
- **parts that are seen and that change its numbers**: tyres (all-terrain, mud), a lift,
  a bull bar, a snorkel (wades deeper), a winch, a roof rack, a light bar, a long-range
  tank, lockers. Each part is bought at the store or the workshop and is a thing until it
  is fitted. Parts are made here, in Blender by script as the shed is, to fit any truck by
  a few points marked on each model.

**Other people's models** were looked at first (a catalogue of 356 downloadable ones is
at https://claude.ai/artifact/FHpTVwMXSyqub2t5RsNR3b) and set aside: their licences, who
really made them and whose badges they wear are all somebody else's, and none comes in
pieces. The Raptor is the one such model in the game, and one of the fifty, the Kestrel,
is there to take its place.

**A truck is its own file.** The Raptor is 97 MB of the file the maps share. With many
trucks, each is baked to a file of its own, and only the one being driven is read beside
the map (`solstice_asset::join` already joins files; the game joins a third).

### Fuel, and the truck's state

- Fuel is used by how hard the engine works. It is bought at the store's bowser; jerry
  cans are things in the tray. Run dry and it is a walk, or a tow that goes on the debt.
- Damage is kept simple: tyres (a puncture needs the spare, which is a thing), suspension
  from hard landings, water in the engine from a ford taken too deep. Each is a bill, and
  until it is paid a truck that drives worse.

### Money

- **Gold** is the income. In dollars that look real: by the gram at about today's price
  ($150), so that a week's finds are measured against a $1,850 Friday (open question 4).
  The aim: an ordinary day early on clears $400 to $800 after fuel and food, a good day
  late on $5,000, and the Lost Reef's ground is worth weeks.
- **Jobs** from the store's noticeboard, which use the truck and its tray: a load of fence
  posts taken to a camp without losing any, a broken trailer brought down from Blackwood,
  the fords on Wombat Creek checked after a storm. They pay less than good gold and never
  fail to pay.
- **Purses** from timed runs (below): paid in gold, once each in full.
- **Costs**: food, fuel, repairs, parts, the truck, the block.

### The calendar

*Asked for (2026-11-15): a calendar with the days, the weather, and when things are due.*

A page of the pause menu and of the shed's card (`apps/viewer/src/calendar.rs`, pure
rules; the page is `calendar` in `ui/`). Four weeks, Monday to Sunday, the week it is at
the top.

- **The days.** Day 1 is Monday 4 May, late autumn. A day's square has its date, its
  weather, and what falls due on it. Today is marked; days gone are dimmed and show the
  weather they had.
- **The weather** comes from the story's seed (`seed N` in the kept game, drawn when a game
  is first started). Fronts come through every four to seven days: the day before one is
  overcast, the day it arrives is a storm or, a weak one, overcast, and the days between
  are clear or fair. They come closer together as the wet sets in, from about day 40.
- **The forecast.** Tomorrow and the day after are the *forecast*, right about nine times
  in ten. The four days after that are the *outlook*, right about two times in three, and
  drawn fainter. Past that nothing is shown. What a forecast gets wrong, it gets wrong by
  one step (fair for overcast, overcast for a storm), and it is the same forecast every
  time it is looked at on the same day.
- **What is due.** Every Friday, each loan's repayment (the block $1,500, the truck $350),
  with the week's total. The next due date is said in words beside the grid, with how many
  days off it is. Jobs with a day to be done by are marked the same way when there are
  jobs.
- At a trailhead today's weather is the one already picked. In free play it can still be
  changed; in the story it is what there is.

Built (2026-11-15): the page, the weather from the seed and the forecast, and the Fridays
with the two loans' repayments on them. The loans themselves are step 2 of the order
below: until then the calendar says what will be due and nothing is taken.

## Timed runs

*Asked for (2026-11-15): each track has classes, each with an icon; bronze, silver and
gold times the game works out; practice; other 4x4s that run the track and can be watched,
as a cinematic that can be skipped, to beat their times; gold for beating the track, in
full only for first wins, and more at night or in the rain.*

This is in both ways of playing. In free play any class can be run in any truck. In the
story a truck runs in the class of its grade, so the classes open as trucks are bought.

### Classes

A class is a grade of truck (`apps/viewer/src/timed.rs`, `CLASSES`). Each has an icon, a
truck of its kind seen from the side, drawn in `ui/index.html` with the rest
(`#class-1` to `#class-5`).

| Class | | The icon | Pays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Paddock bashers | A short tray-back ute | x 1 |
| 2 | Old tourers | A square wagon with a spare on the back | x 1.5 |
| 3 | Workhorses | A dual-cab ute | x 2.5 |
| 4 | Built tourers | A lifted wagon with a rack and a bar | x 4 |
| 5 | The top end | A long, wide desert truck | x 6 |

### Bronze, silver and gold

Nobody types a time in. When a map is entered the game reads its track and works out how
long a good driver in a truck of each class takes to get round it
(`crates/solstice-game/src/par.rs`, `Profile`). It goes along the track two metres at a
time and asks how fast each place can be taken:

- **bends**, from how tight they are and how hard the class corners;
- **what is underfoot**, from the map of the ground: a ford or a bog at a crawl, rock a
  little faster, mud faster again, each a little quicker in a better truck;
- **dips**, a washout or a step, at a crawl;
- **climbs and drops**: the steeper, the slower;
- and no faster than the class's top speed.

Then it works back from every slow place by how hard the class brakes, and forward by how
hard it pulls away, and adds up the time. That is the **gold** time. **Silver** is 12%
slower and **bronze** 30% slower. A track that is changed, or a new one, has its medals
the next time it is entered; the times are kept with the game (`par MAP ...`) only so a
trailhead's card can show them before the map is read.

| Class | Corners (m/s²) | Brakes | Pulls away | Top speed | Crawls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2.6 | 3.2 | 1.2 | 47 km/h | x 1 |
| 2 | 3.0 | 3.6 | 1.5 | 54 km/h | x 1.1 |
| 3 | 3.4 | 4.2 | 1.9 | 65 km/h | x 1.25 |
| 4 | 3.9 | 4.8 | 2.3 | 76 km/h | x 1.4 |
| 5 | 4.5 | 5.5 | 2.8 | 90 km/h | x 1.6 |

These are to be tuned by playing: one number for a class moves every medal on every map.

### Three ways to drive a track

A trailhead's card offers them, with the class, the time of day and the weather.

- **Free roam.** No clock. As now: get out, detect, come back to where the truck was left.
- **Practice.** The clock runs, lap after lap, from the line. The medal times are shown
  and so is how far ahead of or behind each you are, as you go. Nothing is paid and no
  medal is kept: only your best lap.
- **Timed run.** The one that counts. One lap, from a rolling start: the truck is put a
  little behind the line and the clock starts as it crosses. At the end a result: the
  time, the medal, and what was paid. Being put back on the track (the reset button, or a
  truck that fell or was stuck) ends the run's chance of a medal: it is finished for the
  time, and pays nothing.

Before either timed kind, on the map, is the **line-up**: the class, the three times, who
set them, what the run would pay as things are, and **Start**.

### The others

Every class of every track has three other drivers, drawn from the map's name and the
class so that they are always the same three: a name, a truck of that grade, a colour.
One holds the bronze time, one the silver, one the gold. Their times are the medal times.

- **Watching.** From the line-up, any of the three can be watched: a cinematic of their
  run. It is not the whole lap, which is five minutes of somebody else driving: it is the
  start, the slowest place in each third of the track (a ford, a bog, a step: where time
  is won and lost) and the run to the line, a few seconds each, from a camera standing on
  the track ahead of the truck, with their clock reading as it would there. Any button
  skips it.
- **On the track with you.** Timed or practising, the three are on the little map as
  coloured dots, where each would be by now, and the panel says how far ahead of or behind
  each you are.
- **In free roam** they are to be met on the track, going round at their own pace.

Built (2026-11-15): the three drivers and their times, the dots and the gaps, and the
cinematic. In the cinematic the truck that is driven is the map's own, in the other
driver's colour, driven by the game (`drive::autopilot`) at their pace: the game draws one
truck. Their own trucks, on the track at the same time as yours and in free roam, wait for
a truck being a file of its own (step 3 of the order below).

### What a run pays

*Asked for: gold for beating the track; the original money only for first wins; more at
night or in the rain.*

- Each medal of each class on each track has a **purse**. Gold's is $1 for every second of
  the gold time, times what the class pays, to the nearest $10; silver's is half of it and
  bronze's a quarter. (Wombat Creek as it is baked, class 1, gold in 14:42: $880, $440 and
  $220. Class 5, gold in 9:24: $3,380, $1,690 and $850. Every medal of every class on it
  comes to about $17,000.)
- A purse is paid **in full once**: the first time that medal is won. A first gold with no
  medal yet pays all three.
- A run that wins nothing new pays a **tenth** of the purse of the medal it reached.
- **At night** a run pays half as much again; **in a storm**, half as much again; both,
  double. It is on whatever the run pays, a first win or a repeat, so a first gold is
  worth waiting for a wet night for.
- It is paid **in gold**: weighed out to the gram at the buyer's price and put in the tray
  as a nugget, to be driven to town and sold like any other, and lost like any other if it
  is bounced out on the way. A truck with no tray is paid in money.
- What is kept (`run MAP CLASS MEDAL SECONDS` in the kept game): the best medal and the
  best time of each class on each map.

## What is built (15 November 2026)

- Three kept games and a free drive that keeps nothing.
- The debt on the block and a first truck on finance, chosen and then given its colour;
  the bank (borrow, pay off, Friday's repayment, a credit rating); the calendar, which
  shows what the bank will take.
- Fifty trucks in five grades, each bare with a kit of parts; the garage at the shed; the
  auto shop, the used trucks and the bank behind doors in the town.
- Parts change how a truck drives, and so does oil that is overdue.
- Gold: found with the detector, carried, kept behind a truck's doors or in the chest, sold
  by weight in the town.
- Timed runs in the class of the truck's grade, with medals, purses, the others to watch,
  and more paid at night and in the rain.
- The town down its own road from a gate by the shed; a gate home on every track.

Not built: food, fuel and damage, jobs, travel time, repossession, the Lost Reef. The medal
times and every price are untuned: they want driving, and what `save/game.log` says of the
runs that are driven (`timed: class N round MAP in M:SS`).

## The order to build it in

Each step is playable on its own and is put on the site when it works.

1. **Two games and a locked clock.** Story and Free play, each with its own kept game. In
   the story: its own clock, no choosing time or weather, travel time, weather from the
   seed with a forecast.
2. **Debt and food.** The two loans, Fridays, the credit rating, the bills page, the
   credit union's counter. Food at the store; the lost day and the $100.
3. **A truck is its own file, with a colour.** The Raptor moved out of the shared file;
   paint the game can set; the garage's colour page.
4. **The first trucks.** Three from grade 1 in the game: their files, their numbers, the
   choice on the first day, the dealer and what your credit will finance.
5. **Fuel and damage.**
6. **Parts.** The garage's parts, made here; the winch.
7. **More trucks**, a grade at a time, and their insides.
8. **Jobs**, and their cargo.
9. **The Lost Reef.**
10. **Tuning**: what gold is worth, what things cost, how long a day is.

## Questions still open

1. **Events.** Is working your way up from debt enough of a story, or do you want things
   that happen to you along the way: a flood, a mining company pegging ground, an offer
   for the block?
2. **Real names.** Real makes and models by name with their badges on, or our own names
   on real trucks? (It matters most if the game is ever sold.)
3. **Missed Fridays.** Nothing ends the story. As planned a truck two payments behind is
   repossessed and the block is never taken. Is that right, or should nothing ever be
   taken, and missing a payment only cost credit and money?
4. **Gold's price.** By the gram at about $150, with nuggets weighing what real ones do
   (a few grams to a few ounces, rarely more), or the big nuggets and round numbers there
   are now?
5. **Length of a day.** 48 minutes, with repayments every Friday.
6. **Classes.** By grade of truck, as built. Or by kind (utes, wagons, built trucks)
   across grades?
7. **A reset in a timed run.** As built it costs the run its medal. Or a time penalty
   (fifteen seconds, say) and the run still counts?
8. **Repeats.** A tenth of the purse for a run that wins nothing new, or nothing at all?
9. **The purse as a nugget.** Paid in gold in the tray, to be carried to town and sold.
   Or straight into the bank?
10. **How far ahead the calendar sees.** Two days of forecast and four of outlook, as
    built, or only the two days the first plan gave the radio?
11. **The medal times** are from numbers nobody has driven against yet: the class table
    wants tuning with a controller in hand.
